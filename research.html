Bayesian Inverse Problems & Uncertainty Quantification

Observed movement gives us evidence about muscle activity, but it does not determine a unique recruitment pattern. My doctoral research treats this ambiguity as a Bayesian inverse problem: estimate plausible muscle forces and activations while describing uncertainty in those estimates.

Using a computational model of movement, I investigated how physiological prior assumptions shape the inferred recruitment patterns. Markov chain Monte Carlo sampling explores the posterior distributions rather than reducing the answer to one optimal solution. This connects mathematical inference with questions about variability in human motor control.

Quantum Computing & Algorithm Development

Getting classical data into a quantum computation is itself an algorithmic challenge. My collaborative research explores representations and circuit constructions that balance encoding capacity with the resources needed to prepare and process the data.

QPIXL studies multidimensional image representation and compression. QCrank and QBArt extend this research direction to vectorized data encoding and computation on trapped-ion and transmon processors. I applied linear algebra to quantum circuit design as part of the collaborative development of these methods.

Hybrid Quantum-Classical Machine Learning

During my 2023 internship at IonQ, I explored quantum neural networks that combine classical computation with parameterized quantum circuits. Working on a customized model in Python gave me experience investigating how the two computational settings can interact in a learning workflow.

This was exploratory internship research. It deepened my interest in the practical choices behind emerging methods: how to formulate a problem, evaluate a model and communicate what the evidence supports.
